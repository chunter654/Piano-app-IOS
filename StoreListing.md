# App Store listing

The text of the store listing, kept here rather than only in App Store
Connect. The app is priced at $1.99 with no purchases inside it, which the
description says plainly, because "no ads, no pop-ups" is worth more to the
kind of person who buys a piano app than any feature in the list.

The licence on the piano sound obliges us to credit it wherever the app is
distributed, and a credit that lives only in a web form is one nobody can
check. Field limits are Apple's; `Tools/check-listing.sh` measures them and
fails if the credit goes missing.

## Description (4000)

A piano for the iPhone, and nothing else. It opens on two octaves of a grand
piano and plays the moment you touch it. There is no sign-in, no lesson plan
and no feed of songs to unlock.

No ads, no pop-ups, and the ability to play alongside your music or any other
app. You buy it once; there is nothing further to buy inside it.

Two ways to hold it

Tap the brass catch in the corner and the keyboard rearranges. Two stacked
octaves suit the phone held upright, a whole octave to a row. Turn the phone a
quarter turn and the single column suits two hands: pitch runs down the screen
and a brass slider carries you across all 88 keys, from the lowest A to the
top C. Pinch the slider to fit more keys on screen or fewer. Whichever
arrangement you leave it in is the one it opens on next time.

Plays like an instrument

Every finger is tracked separately, so chords sound as chords and a finger
sliding from one key to the next retriggers cleanly rather than sticking. Two
fingers on one key release properly. The note under your finger sounds without
perceptible delay.

Playing alongside another app is the part most piano apps get wrong. This one
layers on top of whatever is already playing, so an album, a podcast or a set
of directions carries on while you play over it, and nothing is interrupted.

One good sound

One grand piano, recorded from a Yamaha C5, and no instrument picker to work
through to reach it. Nothing is streamed and nothing is downloaded, so it
works with no signal at all.

Drawn, not photographed

Warm walnut, aged ivory and muted brass, all generated rather than
photographed, and the naturals are lettered in a serif so you can find middle
C without counting. The screen never rotates: when the keyboard is a single
column it is the instrument that is turned, not the interface, so the keys stay
exactly where your hands left them.
